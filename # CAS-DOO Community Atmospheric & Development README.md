# CAS-DOO: Community Atmospheric & Desalination Stewardship Array

> **Public Domain Notice:** This specification, design framework, and associated documentation are released entirely into the public domain. They are open-source, non-commercial, and free from intellectual property encumbrance to secure water sovereignty for communities worldwide.

---

## 1. System Architecture & Output Specifications

* **Target Daily Volume:** 500 Gallons Per Day (GPD) of high-purity, desalinated fresh water.
* **Active Interfacial Surface Footprint:** 2,000 to 2,200 square feet (modular layout, approx. 40 ft x 50 ft).
* **Source Water Requirement:** Shallow collection trench, natural surface sump, brackish coastal water, or shallow water table access (**0 to 15 feet maximum**). Zero deep-well drilling or heavy industrial machinery required.
* **Energy Profile:** Zero grid reliance. Powered entirely by natural solar thermal gradients and a low-wattage solar-direct trickle pump (or pure gravity head).
* **Dual-Path Capability:** Seamlessly toggle between open-air atmospheric vapor injection (for microclimate and dew-point stabilization) and direct liquid collection (for immediate agricultural or community supply).

---

## 2. The Local Source Logic (Why This Matters)
Even if water is physically present nearby (such as the ocean, a stagnant ditch, or polluted runoff), **presence is not usability**. 

* **Absolute Purification:** Contaminated, brackish, or saltwater passes through the interfacial phase-change layer, leaving behind salt, heavy minerals, bacteria, and chemical contaminants naturally.
* **Zero Infrastructure Burden:** Traditional water treatment requires heavy grid power and complex machinery. This setup uses free solar thermal energy to do the heavy lifting directly at the edge of the source.

## 4. Step-by-Step Assembly Guide

### Step 1: Pick the Right Spot
* Find a flat or gently sloping piece of land that gets plenty of sunlight and is close to your water source (ocean, pond, drainage ditch, or shallow water table up to 15 feet down).

### Step 2: Set Up the Frame
* Assemble the lightweight plastic or metal rails directly on the ground, matching the natural slope of the land. Use earth anchors to keep it secure against the wind. No heavy construction or bulldozers needed.

### Step 3: Lay Down the Solar Absorptive Sheets
* Mount the special solar-evaporation sheets across the top of the frame so they catch maximum sunlight throughout the day. 

### Step 4: Hook Up the Water Feed
* Run a basic hose from your shallow water source to the system using the small solar trickle pump (or let gravity do the work if your source is higher up). 

### Step 5: Choose Your Output Mode
* **Mode A (The Atmosphere):** Let clean vapor vent directly into the air to help humidify dry local microclimates and support local plant life.
* **Mode B (Direct Collection):** Use the built-in underside collection gutters to funnel pure liquid water straight into storage tanks, drinking vessels, or irrigation lines for crops.
